Why Claude Haiku

In November 2026 three cloud models were put through the same tests as the model that runs on a computer with a suitable graphics card. Each was sent the same words and judged by the same code.

Ollama cloud, Gemma 4 31B. The test stopped at its first call. The service did not keep its reply to the form the app asks for, so every pass of a consultation would have failed.

DeepInfra, Gemma 4 26B. A pass of the consultation took 6.5 seconds in the middle, and 241 of 1,889 took over 10 seconds. In 12 of 13 letters of one case it wrote units that the note did not give.

Claude Haiku 5.5. 4,685 calls, none failed. A pass took 4.5 seconds in the middle, and one of 1,898 took over 10 seconds. Every emergency alarm fired. Nothing invented reached any of 65 letters.

Claude Haiku costs about three times as much as the second: about 9 US cents for a 15-minute consultation against 3. The wait a patient feels was put before the cost.

One job goes to a second model. On drug names that may have been misheard, Claude Haiku marked too many names that were written rightly: 244, where the model on the computer marked 27. Claude Sonnet 5.5 made 32 such marks and caught more of the wrong names, 108 of 122. So Claude Sonnet is asked once, at Stop, for the drug names alone. That call costs under one US cent.

What these tests cannot say: which model picks the better differential or asks the better next question. They measure floors and faithfulness to the note, not clinical judgement.
