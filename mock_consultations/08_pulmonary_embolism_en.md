# 08 — Breathlessness with pleuritic pain and a swollen calf: PE (English) — RED-FLAG VARIANT

**Fictional patient:** Mrs. Anusha Peiris, 41, garment factory machine operator
**Speakers:** Doctor, Patient · **Target length:** 3–4 minutes
**Purpose:** urgency-escalation test case. The classic triad is distributed
across the history (breathlessness → pleuritic pain → calf swelling) so the
CDS should escalate progressively as the picture assembles.

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Differential:** pulmonary embolism (leading, with DVT source), pneumonia/pleurisy, musculoskeletal chest pain, cardiac cause
- **Key positives:** sudden breathlessness since yesterday morning, right-sided chest pain that is sharp and worse on deep breathing, right calf swollen/warm/tender for three days, recent overnight bus journey to Jaffna and back, on the combined oral contraceptive pill; exam — RR 24, saturation 92% on air, pulse 110, right calf 3 cm larger than left, warm and tender
- **Key negatives:** no fever, no cough or sputum, no haemoptysis, no previous clots, non-smoker
- **Examination:** RR 24, SpO2 92%, pulse 110 regular, BP 125/80, chest clear on auscultation, right calf swollen warm tender

## Expected urgent_actions (marking scheme for the urgency evaluation)

- **Should fire:** YES — pleuritic chest pain + breathlessness + hypoxia + unilateral leg swelling + OCP + travel is suspected PE with DVT; time-critical
- **Expected content:** emergency hospital transfer / same-day admission for suspected pulmonary embolism (CTPA pathway, anticoagulation); oxygen if available
- **Expected first fire:** by the time the calf swelling joins the breathlessness/pleuritic pain (roughly turns 10–16), possibly earlier on hypoxia
- **Expected clearing:** the doctor calls an ambulance and gives oxygen at the end — fired actions should clear by the final update

---

**DOCTOR:** Good morning, come in. You look uncomfortable — what's happening?

**PATIENT:** Good morning, doctor. Since yesterday morning I can't breathe properly. Even walking from the gate to here, I'm pulling for air.

**DOCTOR:** Did it come on suddenly or build up slowly?

**PATIENT:** Suddenly, doctor. Yesterday morning I was hanging the washing and suddenly it was like I couldn't fill my chest.

**DOCTOR:** Any pain?

**PATIENT:** Yes — here, the right side. A sharp pain, like a knife. When I take a deep breath it stabs, so I'm taking small breaths.

**DOCTOR:** Does it hurt when you press on the spot? Or when you twist?

**PATIENT:** Not really pressing, no. Only with the breathing. Coughing also hurts — but I don't have much of a cough.

**DOCTOR:** Any fever? Chills? Bringing up phlegm?

**PATIENT:** No fever, doctor. No phlegm. It's not like a cold at all.

**DOCTOR:** Have you coughed up any blood, even streaks?

**PATIENT:** No, no blood.

**DOCTOR:** Alright. Anything else bothering you — anything at all, even if it seems unconnected?

**PATIENT:** Well... my right leg, doctor. The calf has been swollen since about three days. It's paining to walk and it feels warm. I thought it was from the bus — we went to Jaffna for a family thing, overnight bus both ways, last week. Squeezed in the seat the whole night.

**DOCTOR:** The calf came first, then the breathing?

**PATIENT:** Yes — leg from about Thursday, breathing from yesterday.

**DOCTOR:** Important question — do you take the family planning pill? The daily one?

**PATIENT:** Yes, doctor, for about five years now.

**DOCTOR:** Any previous blood clots — in the legs or lungs? Anyone in the family?

**PATIENT:** No, nothing like that. Nobody that I know of.

**DOCTOR:** Do you smoke?

**PATIENT:** No, never.

**DOCTOR:** Let me examine you now. I'm counting your breaths... you're taking 24 a minute — fast. This clip on your finger... oxygen is 92 percent — lower than it should be; a healthy reading is 96 or above. Pulse 110, regular but fast. Blood pressure 125 over 80, that's fine. Let me listen to your chest... breathe as deeply as the pain allows... air entry is equal, no crackles, sounds surprisingly clear. Now the legs — yes, the right calf is clearly swollen; I'm measuring... about three centimetres bigger than the left, warm, and tender when I press. The left is normal.

**PATIENT:** What is it, doctor? Did I strain a muscle in the bus?

**DOCTOR:** Mrs. Peiris, I have to take this very seriously, and I'll explain why. I believe there's a blood clot in your right leg — that's the swelling and warmth — and that a piece of it has travelled to your lung. That's called a pulmonary embolism, and it's what's causing the breathlessness, the sharp pain when you breathe, the low oxygen, and the fast pulse. The long cramped bus journey and the pill both make clots more likely.

**PATIENT:** A clot in the lung? Is that dangerous, doctor?

**DOCTOR:** It's treatable — very treatable — but it's an emergency, because more of the clot can travel. You need to be in hospital today, now, not tomorrow. They'll do a special scan of the lungs to confirm it, and start blood-thinning injections straight away. Those injections stop it getting worse, and the body clears the clot with time.

**PATIENT:** Today itself... My husband is at work, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Call him from here, but we're not waiting for him. I'm calling the ambulance now — with your oxygen at 92 you're not going by bus or three-wheeler. While we wait I'm putting you on oxygen, just to make you comfortable. Sit here, don't walk around; keep the leg still.

**PATIENT:** Alright, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** One more thing — the pill: stop it from today; it will have to change to another method, but the hospital and I will guide you on that after this is treated. Now — the oxygen mask first, then I'll ring the ambulance and write your referral. You're going to be fine, but we do this properly and we do it now.

**PATIENT:** Thank you, doctor. I'm glad I came.
