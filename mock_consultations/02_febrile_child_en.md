# 02 — Fever in a child, mother gives history (English)

**Fictional patients:** Sanuki Silva, 6, brought by her mother Mrs. Dilrukshi Silva
**Speakers:** Doctor, Mother (the two recorded voices) · **Target length:** 4–5 minutes

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Differential:** dengue fever (leading, given day 3 fever + retro-orbital pain + local cases), other viral fever, early urinary infection less likely
- **Key positives:** fever 3 days up to 39 °C, headache, pain behind the eyes, vomited twice today, poor appetite, dengue cases in the neighbourhood
- **Key negatives:** no rash, no bleeding (gums/nose/urine/stool), no cough or cold symptoms, no ear pain, passing urine normally, drinking fluids
- **Examination:** temp 38.9 °C, alert, well hydrated, no rash or bleeding spots, throat mildly red, chest clear, abdomen soft with no tenderness and no enlarged liver, capillary refill normal
- **Plan:** full blood count and dengue NS1 antigen today, paracetamol only — **no ibuprofen or other NSAIDs**, fluid intake advice, daily review with repeat FBC, clear dengue warning signs for immediate hospital admission (severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, drowsiness, reduced urine, cold clammy skin — especially as fever settles)

---

**DOCTOR:** Good morning, come in. And who is this? Hello!

**MOTHER:** This is Sanuki, doctor. She's six. She's had fever for three days now and I'm getting worried.

**DOCTOR:** Alright, tell me from the beginning.

**MOTHER:** It started Friday evening. She came home from school looking tired, and by night she was hot. We checked with the thermometer — 39 it said. It comes down a bit with paracetamol but then it comes back up.

**DOCTOR:** So today is the third day. How is she in herself — playing, talking?

**MOTHER:** When the fever comes she just lies down. She says her head hurts, and this morning she said her eyes hurt — behind the eyes, she said.

**DOCTOR:** Pain behind the eyes. Any vomiting?

**MOTHER:** Twice this morning, doctor. And she doesn't want to eat anything.

**DOCTOR:** Is she drinking? Water, king coconut, anything?

**MOTHER:** She drinks a little when I force her. Not like usual.

**DOCTOR:** Passing urine as normal?

**MOTHER:** Yes, this morning also she went.

**DOCTOR:** Any rash on the body? Any bleeding — from the gums when brushing, nose bleeds, blood in the urine or motions?

**MOTHER:** No, nothing like that. I checked her whole body this morning — no rash.

**DOCTOR:** Cough, cold, sore throat, ear pain?

**MOTHER:** A very small cough, hardly anything. She hasn't complained about the ears.

**DOCTOR:** Now, important question — anyone in the area with dengue recently?

**MOTHER:** Yes, doctor, that's why I'm frightened. Two houses down, the boy was admitted with dengue last week. And there's a construction site near us with water collecting.

**DOCTOR:** I understand. Has Sanuki had dengue before, or any other illnesses? Any medicines regularly, any allergies?

**MOTHER:** No dengue before. She's a healthy child. No allergies. Only the paracetamol I've been giving.

**DOCTOR:** Good. Sanuki, I'm going to check you now, is that okay? First the temperature... 38.9, so the fever is there now. Let me look at your eyes... a little red. Open your mouth wide — aah — throat is slightly red, nothing much. Let me listen to your chest... breathe in... good girl... chest is completely clear. Now lie down, I'll press your tummy gently... does that hurt? No? Tummy is soft, no tenderness, liver is not enlarged. Let me look at your skin... no rash, no bleeding spots. And I'll press your fingernail... colour comes back nicely, so her circulation is good. She's alert and she's well hydrated.

**MOTHER:** Is it dengue, doctor?

**DOCTOR:** It could be, and with day-three fever, eye pain, vomiting, and dengue in your lane, we must check today. It may still be an ordinary viral fever — the examination is reassuring — but we don't guess with dengue. I'm sending her now for a full blood count and a dengue NS1 test. The lab is just next door; results by this evening.

**MOTHER:** Alright, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Now listen carefully, because this part is important. Fever medicine: paracetamol ONLY. Do not give ibuprofen, or anything like Brufen — no other fever medicines at all. If it's dengue, those can cause bleeding.

**MOTHER:** Only paracetamol. Yes, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Fluids are the main treatment right now. Small amounts, often — water, king coconut, kanji, jeewani if she'll take it. Don't worry about solid food for now; worry about drinks.

**MOTHER:** I'll manage that.

**DOCTOR:** I want to see her again tomorrow with the blood report — daily checks until she's better, because with dengue the blood count can change quickly. And take her STRAIGHT to hospital — day or night — if any of these happen: bad tummy pain, vomiting that won't stop, any bleeding at all, if she becomes very sleepy or confused, if she stops passing urine, or if her hands and feet become cold and clammy. Even if the fever has gone down — especially if the fever has gone down and she seems worse. That's the dangerous time with dengue.

**MOTHER:** Straight to hospital. I understand, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Very good. She's stable now, and you're doing all the right things. Do the blood test, push the fluids, and I'll see you both tomorrow.

**MOTHER:** Thank you very much, doctor. Say thank you, Sanuki!

**DOCTOR:** See you tomorrow, Sanuki. Feel better!
