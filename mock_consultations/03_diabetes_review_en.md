# 03 — Diabetes and blood-pressure review (English)

**Fictional patient:** Mrs. Kamala Fernando, 58, retired teacher, type 2 diabetes for 8 years
**Speakers:** Doctor, Patient · **Target length:** 4–5 minutes

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Problems:** type 2 diabetes with suboptimal control, hypertension, new peripheral neuropathy symptoms, overdue eye screening, statin not yet started
- **Current medication:** metformin 1 g twice daily, gliclazide 80 mg in the morning, losartan 50 mg daily
- **Key positives:** HbA1c 8.4% three months ago, home fasting sugars around 160, burning/tingling in both feet at night for ~2 months, increased thirst, sweet tea twice daily, large rice portions, no regular exercise
- **Key negatives:** no chest pain, no visual complaints, no foot ulcers, no hypoglycaemic episodes
- **Examination:** BP 140/85, weight 68 kg, feet — skin intact, pulses present, reduced monofilament sensation both forefeet
- **Plan:** increase gliclazide to 80 mg twice daily, start atorvastatin at night, repeat HbA1c, creatinine/eGFR, lipid profile, urine ACR, refer for retinal (eye) screening, daily foot inspection advice, dietary changes (smaller rice portion, stop sugar in tea, evening walking), review in 3 months with results

---

**DOCTOR:** Good morning, Mrs. Fernando. Sit down. You're here for the diabetes check-up?

**PATIENT:** Yes, doctor. Three months since the last visit.

**DOCTOR:** How have you been feeling in general?

**PATIENT:** Not too bad, doctor. But I get very thirsty these days. And there's one thing troubling me — my feet. At night, both feet, there's a burning, tingling feeling. Like ants walking. It wakes me up sometimes.

**DOCTOR:** How long has that been going on?

**PATIENT:** About two months now, I'd say.

**DOCTOR:** Both feet equally? Any numbness — like walking on cushions? Any wounds or ulcers on the feet?

**PATIENT:** Both feet, yes. A little numb also. No wounds — I check, my daughter told me to check.

**DOCTOR:** Very good that you check. Now, your sugar control. You're taking metformin one gram in the morning and one gram at night, and gliclazide 80 in the morning — still the same?

**PATIENT:** Yes, doctor, I don't miss them. And the losartan 50 for the pressure.

**DOCTOR:** Any episodes of low sugar — shaking, sweating, feeling faint?

**PATIENT:** No, nothing like that.

**DOCTOR:** Do you check your sugar at home?

**PATIENT:** My son bought a machine. The morning sugar, before eating, is usually around 160. Sometimes 170.

**DOCTOR:** And your last HbA1c — the three-month average test — was 8.4. We want it below 7, ideally. So the fasting readings and the HbA1c both tell the same story — the control isn't where it should be. Tell me about food. Be honest now!

**PATIENT:** *(laughs)* Doctor, I eat normally. Rice in the afternoon, maybe bread or string hoppers at night.

**DOCTOR:** How much rice? Show me with your hands.

**PATIENT:** ...A good plate, doctor. And tea with sugar — two spoons, twice a day. I know, I know.

**DOCTOR:** *(laughs)* You said it before I did. Any exercise? Walking?

**PATIENT:** Not really. My knees, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Chest pain, breathlessness on walking? Any trouble with your eyes — blurring?

**PATIENT:** No chest pain. Eyes are alright, but it's been more than a year since the eye check.

**DOCTOR:** We'll fix that today. Let me examine you now. Blood pressure first... 140 over 85. Better than last time, but with diabetes I'd like it a bit lower. Weight... 68 kilos, same as before. Now the feet — shoes and socks off, please. Skin looks healthy, no cracks, no ulcers. Pulses... I can feel them, both feet, good. Now close your eyes — tell me when you feel this thin filament touching... here?... here?... and here?

**PATIENT:** ...I felt the ankle one. The toe ones I couldn't feel much, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** That's what I suspected. Mrs. Fernando, the burning and the reduced feeling — this is diabetic nerve damage, neuropathy. It happens when the sugar has run high over years. It's the body telling us to tighten the control, now, before it gets worse.

**PATIENT:** Can it be cured, doctor?

**DOCTOR:** The damage done is hard to undo, but good control stops it progressing, and the burning often improves. So today we make changes. One — I'm increasing the gliclazide to 80 in the morning AND 80 at night. Metformin stays the same. Two — I'm starting a cholesterol tablet, atorvastatin, one at night. With diabetes we protect the heart early; this is standard, not because something new is wrong.

**PATIENT:** Alright, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Three — tests before the next visit: the HbA1c, kidney test, cholesterol, and a urine test for protein — that checks whether diabetes is touching the kidneys. Four — the eye screening. I'm writing the referral now; they photograph the back of the eye. More than a year is too long with diabetes.

**PATIENT:** I'll go, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** And the food. Rice down to half of what you take now — fill the space with vegetables, gotukola, mallum, dhal. The sugar in the tea — stop it completely. That alone is a spoonful of sugar straight into the blood four times a day. And walking — with the knees, start small: fifteen minutes in the evening, flat road, proper shoes.

**PATIENT:** No sugar at all, doctor? Plain tea is so sad.

**DOCTOR:** *(laughs)* You'll get used to it in two weeks, I promise. Your feet: check them every day, like your daughter said — top, bottom, between the toes. Any wound, any colour change — come immediately, don't wait.

**PATIENT:** Every day, yes.

**DOCTOR:** So: gliclazide twice daily now, new cholesterol tablet at night, blood and urine tests, eye referral, and I'll see you in three months with all the results. Any questions?

**PATIENT:** No, doctor, it's all clear. Thank you.

**DOCTOR:** Good. And Mrs. Fernando — the plain tea. I'll ask next time!
