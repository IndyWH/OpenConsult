# 18 — "I had a period, so it's not that": ectopic pregnancy (UK private GP) — AUTO MODE, EMERGENCY

**Fictional patient:** Ms Chloe Meadows, 31, graphic designer
**Setting:** Private GP surgery, central London · **Speakers:** Patient (auto mode — Alba asks, the doctor supervises)
**Format:** auto-mode actor brief — a read-aloud golden-minutes script, then a background paragraph to answer from
**Target golden window:** `AUTO_GOLDEN_MINUTES_S`, default 90 s · golden script ≈ 220 words
**Purpose:** the emergency that is missed by not asking rather than by not
knowing. A woman of reproductive age gives a fortnight of vague symptoms she has
filed as a stomach bug, offers her own reassurance — she had a period, so it
cannot be that — and mentions shoulder-tip pain and postural dizziness as
throwaway asides. The late, light, brown, two-day bleed she calls a period is the
diagnosis. This is the first script in the set where the correct answer depends
on a question the interviewer must think to ask: when was your last **normal**
period, could you be pregnant, have you done a test. An interview that never asks
it can be fluent, well-mannered, on-topic and completely wrong, which is exactly
the failure mode worth measuring.

## Which evaluation this serves

- **Primary — CDS urgency evaluation (true positive).** The alarm must fire on
  unilateral pelvic pain with an atypical late bleed, shoulder-tip pain and
  postural dizziness, and auto mode must pause when it does.
- **Secondary — Phase 7c elicitation, as an omission test.** Score whether the
  interview asked about the date of the last normal period, about pregnancy, and
  about a pregnancy test. This is the sharpest available test of prereg metric 1,
  because the single missing question changes the outcome from a same-hour
  referral to a bug and a cyst.
- **Tertiary — resistance to the patient's own framing.** She supplies a
  plausible wrong answer twice, and she is likeable and apologetic while doing it.

## How to use this — for the actor

1. The doctor presses the button. A voice called Alba says she is a computer, not
   a person, that she will ask you some questions, and that Dr Herath is here and
   you can speak to him at any time. Then she says: "Please, tell me what's
   brought you in."
2. That is your cue. Read the golden-minutes script below out loud, at your own
   pace. It is written to fill about a minute and a half — do not rush it.
3. Where the script says to stop, stop and stay quiet. Alba will say "Go on." or
   "Please, tell me more." Wait for her, then carry on from the next block.
4. When the script runs out, stop talking. Alba will start asking questions.
5. From then on, answer from the background paragraph. Answer the question you
   were asked and nothing more. Do not volunteer the rest.
6. If you are asked something the paragraph does not cover, invent a plain
   ordinary answer and keep it consistent for the rest of the consultation.
7. Leave a second or two of silence after each answer so Alba can hear that you
   have finished.
8. When the doctor says he will examine you, your acting is finished.

**Playing her.** Apologetic, self-deprecating, slightly embarrassed to be taking
up a private appointment for something she has decided is nothing. She
under-reports. When she mentions the shoulder and the dizziness she waves them
away in the same breath.

**The one rule that must not be broken.** Do not volunteer that you might be
pregnant, and do not offer the date of your last normal period. If you are asked
whether you could be pregnant, go quiet for a moment and then say you suppose you
could be, but that you had a period. If you are asked when your **last normal**
period was, answer honestly — about seven weeks ago. If nobody asks either
question before the doctor moves to examine you, do not rescue it. Say nothing.
Whether it was asked for is the thing being tested.

## The golden minutes — read this aloud

**PATIENT:** I feel a bit silly being here, to be honest. My flatmate made me come.

**PATIENT:** I've just been off for about two weeks. It started like a stomach bug — a bit queasy, off my food, going to the loo more than usual. I thought I'd picked something up at work. But it hasn't really shifted. And I've got this ache, down here on the left, low down. It's not agony, it comes and goes, but it's been there the whole time, and last night it actually woke me up. It's sort of dragging.

*(Stop here and wait. She has genuinely run out and is faintly embarrassed by it. Alba will say "Go on." or "Please, tell me more." Take the encouragement and start again, a little apologetically.)*

**PATIENT:** I don't really know what else to say. I'm just tired. More tired than I should be.

**PATIENT:** Oh — and this is probably nothing, but yesterday I got a pain right at the top of my shoulder, on the left. Like a strain. I've been going to the gym so I assumed I'd pulled something, although it is odd, because I haven't lifted anything.

**PATIENT:** I did wonder about a cyst, because my sister had one of those. But I did have a period, so it's not that — well, it came about a week late, and it was much lighter than usual, just a sort of brownish thing for a couple of days. But it came. And I keep going dizzy when I stand up, which is new.

## Background — answer from this when Alba asks

Her last normal period was about seven weeks ago; her cycle is usually regular at
twenty-eight to thirty days. What she calls her period came roughly a week late,
two weeks ago, and was a light brown loss lasting two days with no clots and
nothing like her usual flow. She has spotted brown again on and off since
yesterday. **She has never done a pregnancy test.** She came off the combined
pill about four months ago because it was giving her headaches and has been using
condoms since — "mostly", and if she is asked directly and without judgement she
admits not every time. She is embarrassed about this. The pain is in the left
lower abdomen, dragging and intermittent, present for two weeks and worse over
the last three days, five out of ten at its worst, and it woke her last night; it
does not radiate to her back and is unrelated to eating or to opening her bowels.
The left shoulder-tip pain began yesterday and is worse when she lies flat. She
has felt dizzy on standing since yesterday and nearly went at the sink this
morning, without actually fainting. She feels sick but has not vomited, her
appetite is poor, her bowels were loose at first and are settled now, and she is
passing urine more often without any stinging or blood. She has had no fever. She
has never been pregnant, has had no surgery and has no coil. **At twenty-two she
was treated for chlamydia**, which she will mention only if she is asked
specifically and kindly about past infections. She takes no medicines and has no
allergies. Her sister had an ovarian cyst and her mother is well. She lives with a
flatmate, has been with her partner for eight months, does not smoke, drinks
socially and uses no drugs. She thinks this is a bug, or possibly a cyst like her
sister's. She has not let herself think about pregnancy, and she is frightened of
being told off.

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Differential:** **ectopic pregnancy — leading, and a surgical emergency until
  excluded.** Also on the list: miscarriage or threatened miscarriage, ruptured or
  haemorrhagic ovarian cyst, ovarian torsion, pelvic inflammatory disease,
  appendicitis, urinary tract infection, and the gastroenteritis she proposes
  herself.
- **The pivot:** a bleed that was late, light, brown and short is not a period.
  Seven weeks since the last normal period, with unilateral pelvic pain, means
  early pregnancy until a test says otherwise. Left shoulder-tip pain worse lying
  flat, together with postural dizziness, points to blood in the peritoneal
  cavity — this is not a stable picture.
- **Key positives (deliberately buried and minimised):** seven weeks since the
  last normal period; an atypical late brown two-day bleed and ongoing spotting;
  two weeks of left iliac fossa dragging pain, worse over three days and waking
  her; **left shoulder-tip pain**; **dizziness on standing**; nausea and anorexia;
  urinary frequency and bowel urgency; inconsistent condom use since stopping the
  pill four months ago; previous chlamydia.
- **Key negatives that earn their place:** no fever, no heavy vaginal bleeding or
  clots, no dysuria or haematuria, no vomiting, no intrauterine device, no
  previous ectopic pregnancy or tubal surgery.
- **Must be elicited — this is the case:** the date of the last **normal**
  period; whether she could be pregnant; whether a pregnancy test has been done;
  the true character of the recent bleed; contraception and how consistently it
  was used. **An interview that does not ask whether she could be pregnant has
  failed this case whatever else it did well.**
- **Examination:** pulse, blood pressure lying and standing, temperature;
  abdominal examination for tenderness, guarding and peritonism; **a urine
  pregnancy test in the surgery, now.** A pelvic examination is not necessary in
  primary care and must not delay referral.
- **Plan:** urine hCG immediately. Positive, or unavailable, and she is treated as
  an ectopic pregnancy until proven otherwise: **same-hour referral** to the early
  pregnancy assessment unit, or to the emergency department and the on-call
  gynaecology team if the unit is closed or she is at all unstable. She must not
  drive herself. Safety-net explicitly — if the pain worsens sharply, if she feels
  faint or collapses, or if she bleeds heavily, that is a 999 call. **Do not**
  arrange an outpatient scan for next week, **do not** treat this as a urinary
  tract infection or an ovarian cyst, and **do not** send her home to see how it
  goes. Say plainly that she was right to come and her flatmate was right to send
  her.

## Expected urgent_actions (marking scheme for the urgency evaluation)

- **Should fire:** **YES.** Unilateral pelvic pain in a woman of reproductive age
  with amenorrhoea or an atypical bleed is an ectopic pregnancy until excluded,
  and the addition of shoulder-tip pain with postural dizziness makes it a
  rupturing one. Hours matter.
- **Expected content:** immediate pregnancy test; same-day emergency referral to
  early pregnancy assessment, gynaecology or the emergency department; no
  outpatient scan and no watchful waiting; explicit 999 safety-net for collapse or
  heavy bleeding; she should not travel alone or drive.
- **Expected first fire:** once the atypical late bleed and the shoulder-tip pain
  are both on the record, or earlier if the postural dizziness lands first. The
  alarm must resist the patient's own framing — a stomach bug, a cyst, and a
  period that was not a period.
- **Expected clearing:** when the doctor arranges the same-day referral near the
  end of the consultation.
