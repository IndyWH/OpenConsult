# 01 — Chest pain on exertion (English)

**Fictional patient:** Mr. Ranjith Perera, 52, three-wheeler driver
**Speakers:** Doctor, Patient · **Target length:** 4–5 minutes read aloud

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Differential:** stable angina / possible ACS (leading), GORD, musculoskeletal pain
- **Key positives:** exertional central chest tightness ×2 weeks, radiation to left arm once, smoker 20/day, father had heart attack at 60, untreated high blood pressure
- **Key negatives:** no pain at rest, no breathlessness at rest, no chest wall tenderness
- **Examination:** BP 150/95, pulse 88 regular, heart sounds normal, lungs clear
- **Plan:** ECG today, urgent referral for cardiology work-up (troponin, exercise testing), start aspirin after ECG, strong smoking-cessation advice, safety-netting — go straight to hospital if pain at rest >15 min

---

**DOCTOR:** Good morning, come in, have a seat. What brings you in today?

**PATIENT:** Good morning, doctor. I've been getting a pain in my chest, here in the middle. It's been about two weeks now.

**DOCTOR:** I see. Tell me more about this pain — what does it feel like?

**PATIENT:** Like a tightness, doctor. Like something heavy pressing on my chest.

**DOCTOR:** And when does it come on?

**PATIENT:** Mostly when I'm doing something. Yesterday I was pushing the three-wheeler to the shed, and it came on badly. When I climb the stairs at home also.

**DOCTOR:** How long does it last?

**PATIENT:** Maybe five minutes, ten minutes. If I stop and rest, it goes away.

**DOCTOR:** Does the pain move anywhere — to your arm, your jaw, your back?

**PATIENT:** One time it went to my left arm. That frightened me a bit, to be honest. That's why I came.

**DOCTOR:** You did the right thing coming in. Do you get it at rest — sitting quietly, watching television?

**PATIENT:** No, no. Only when I'm exerting.

**DOCTOR:** Any breathlessness with it? Sweating, feeling sick?

**PATIENT:** A little out of breath when the pain comes. No vomiting, nothing like that.

**DOCTOR:** Any burning feeling, like acid coming up? Does it get worse after meals or when lying down?

**PATIENT:** Sometimes after a big rice packet I get a burning, but this pressing pain is different. This is new.

**DOCTOR:** Does it hurt if you press on your chest, or when you twist your body?

**PATIENT:** No, doctor, I tried pressing. Nothing.

**DOCTOR:** Now, some questions about your health generally. Do you smoke?

**PATIENT:** Yes... about a packet a day. Twenty. For thirty years, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Alcohol?

**PATIENT:** Weekends only. Two, three glasses.

**DOCTOR:** Any medical problems you know of — diabetes, blood pressure, cholesterol?

**PATIENT:** Last year at a medical camp they said my pressure was high. They told me to see a doctor, but I never came. I feel fine usually, so.

**DOCTOR:** And in the family? Heart attacks, strokes?

**PATIENT:** My father had a heart attack. He was about sixty when it happened.

**DOCTOR:** Any medicines you take regularly? Any allergies?

**PATIENT:** Nothing, doctor. No allergies that I know.

**DOCTOR:** Alright. I'd like to examine you now. I'll check your pulse first... your pulse is 88 and regular. Now the blood pressure... that's 150 over 95 — it is high, like they told you at the camp. Let me listen to your heart... heart sounds are normal, no murmurs. Take a deep breath for me... again... your lungs are clear. And pressing here on the chest wall — any pain?

**PATIENT:** No pain, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Good. Now, Mr. Perera, let me explain what I'm thinking. This pattern — a tightness that comes with effort and settles with rest, going to the left arm, in a smoker with high blood pressure and your father's history — I'm concerned this could be angina. That means the blood vessels supplying your heart may be narrowed.

**PATIENT:** Is it a heart attack, doctor?

**DOCTOR:** I don't think you're having a heart attack right now, but this is a warning sign we must take seriously. Here's the plan. We'll do an ECG — a heart tracing — right now, in this room. Then I'm referring you to the cardiology clinic urgently for blood tests and further heart tests. I'll also start you on aspirin, a small dose daily, once we've done the tracing.

**PATIENT:** Alright, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Two more things, and they matter as much as the medicine. The smoking has to stop — completely. This is exactly how it damages the heart. And we need to treat that blood pressure; we'll confirm it at the next visit and likely start a tablet.

**PATIENT:** I've been trying to cut down...

**DOCTOR:** Cutting down isn't enough with what's happening. We have clinics that can help you stop — I'll give you the details. Now, very important: if you get this pain at rest, lasting more than fifteen minutes, and not settling — don't wait, don't come here — go straight to the hospital, to the emergency department. Understood?

**PATIENT:** Straight to hospital. Understood, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Good. Let's do that ECG now, and I'll see you afterwards with the referral letter.

**PATIENT:** Thank you, doctor.
