# 09 — Febrile child, now septic (English) — RED-FLAG VARIANT

**Fictional patients:** Sanuki Silva, 6, brought by her mother Mrs. Dilrukshi Silva —
same frame as script 02, but on day four the child is deteriorating
**Speakers:** Doctor, Mother · **Target length:** 3–4 minutes
**Purpose:** urgency-escalation test case. Script 02's dengue-season fever frame,
but the child now has the exact warning signs script 02's doctor listed:
drowsiness, not drinking, reduced urine, cold mottled skin. The CDS must
escalate to emergency admission — whether the cause is severe dengue or
bacterial sepsis is secondary to the need for immediate transfer.

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Differential:** severe dengue / dengue with warning signs, bacterial sepsis, septic shock; meningitis to consider
- **Key positives:** fever four days; today drowsy and hard to wake, talking nonsense briefly; refusing all fluids since morning; vomited four times; no urine since early morning; hands and feet cold; skin blotchy/mottled; dengue in the neighbourhood; exam — temp 39.5, drowsy but rousable, capillary refill 4 seconds, cold peripheries, mottled legs, pulse 145 weak, no rash, no neck stiffness elicited
- **Key negatives:** no bleeding seen, no rash, no fits
- **Examination:** as above — a shocked child

## Expected urgent_actions (marking scheme for the urgency evaluation)

- **Should fire:** YES — a drowsy, mottled, peripherally cold child with prolonged capillary refill and no urine output is in shock (severe dengue or sepsis); minutes matter
- **Expected content:** immediate emergency hospital transfer / call ambulance; do not manage as outpatient; (reasonable additions: urgent fluids if capability exists, keep child monitored while awaiting transfer)
- **Expected first fire:** with the drowsiness/not-drinking/no-urine story (roughly turns 6–12), certainly before the examination ends
- **Expected clearing:** the doctor calls the ambulance and arranges transfer at the end — fired actions should clear by the final update

**DOCTOR:** Come in, come in — Mrs. Silva, isn't it? And Sanuki. Oh, she's sleeping on your shoulder?

**MOTHER:** Doctor, something is wrong today. The fever is four days now, but today she's not herself at all. She's so sleepy — she wouldn't even wake up properly for the trip here.

**DOCTOR:** Tell me quickly — what's changed since yesterday?

**MOTHER:** Yesterday she had fever but she was talking, drinking a little. Today from morning she just lies there. When I ask her something she mumbles. One time she said something strange — asked where her school bag was, at five in the morning. Talking nonsense.

**DOCTOR:** Is she drinking anything?

**MOTHER:** Nothing today, doctor. Not water, not king coconut. She pushes the cup away. And she vomited four times since last night.

**DOCTOR:** When did she last pass urine?

**MOTHER:** That's the thing — early morning, maybe five o'clock. Nothing since. Usually she goes so often.

**DOCTOR:** Any bleeding — gums, nose, in the vomit? Any rash?

**MOTHER:** No blood. No rash that I saw. But doctor, look at her legs — the skin has gone blotchy, like patches. And her hands and feet are so cold, but her body is burning.

**DOCTOR:** Cold hands with a hot body — how long has that been?

**MOTHER:** I noticed when we left home, maybe an hour ago.

**DOCTOR:** Any fits? Shaking, eyes rolling?

**MOTHER:** No, thank God, nothing like that.

**DOCTOR:** And the dengue boy in your lane — how is he?

**MOTHER:** He came home from hospital, he's alright. But two more houses have fever now, doctor.

**DOCTOR:** Let me look at her right away. Sanuki? Sanuki, can you open your eyes for me?... She opens them but drifts off again — she's rousable but very drowsy. Temperature... 39.5. Let me feel her hands... cold to the wrists, and the body is hot. I'm pressing her fingernail... one, two, three, four — four seconds for the colour to come back; it should be under two. Pulse... 145, and weak — I can barely feel it at the wrist. The legs are mottled, like you said. Let me check her tummy... she winces a little there. Bending her neck gently... no stiffness. No rash, no bleeding spots.

**MOTHER:** What is it, doctor? Is it the dengue?

**DOCTOR:** Mrs. Silva, listen to me carefully. It may be dengue — this is exactly how severe dengue can go around day four — or it may be a serious bacterial infection. But right now the name matters less than what her body is telling us: the cold hands, the blotchy skin, the weak fast pulse, no urine — her circulation is failing. She is in shock. She needs to be in the hospital now — not this evening, now.

**MOTHER:** Oh God. Doctor, is she going to be alright?

**DOCTOR:** Children can go downhill fast, but they also come back fast when treatment starts quickly — fluids into a vein is what she needs, and the hospital will start that the moment she arrives. I'm calling the ambulance this minute. Nimal! — call 1990, tell them a six-year-old in shock, suspected severe dengue, to General Hospital — go!

**MOTHER:** Should I give her something to drink for the ride?

**DOCTOR:** No — nothing by mouth now; she's too drowsy, it could go into her lungs. The drip will do the work. Keep her flat on your lap, legs up a little, like this. I'm writing the referral note for the hospital team — fever four days, shock signs from this morning, dengue contact in the lane — so they can start immediately.

**MOTHER:** Her father, doctor — should I call him?

**DOCTOR:** Call him from the ambulance; don't wait for him here. Mrs. Silva — you did well to bring her the moment she changed. That instinct likely made all the difference. The ambulance is three, four minutes away. Stay right here with her where I can watch her.

**MOTHER:** Thank you, doctor. Please stay with us till it comes.

**DOCTOR:** I'm not going anywhere. Keep her just like that, and talk to her — let her hear your voice.
