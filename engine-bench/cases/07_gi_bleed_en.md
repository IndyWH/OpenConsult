# 07 — Dyspepsia with melaena: upper GI bleed (English) — RED-FLAG VARIANT

**Fictional patient:** Mr. Sunil Bandara, 35, delivery driver — same frame as
script 05, but the ulcer is now bleeding
**Speakers:** Doctor, Patient · **Target length:** 3–4 minutes
**Purpose:** urgency-escalation test case. Script 05's routine dyspepsia story
with two red flags added: black tarry stools and postural dizziness. The CDS
must recognise the alarm features inside an otherwise familiar frame.

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme for AI outputs)

- **Differential:** bleeding peptic ulcer / upper GI haemorrhage (leading), NSAID-related gastritis with bleeding, oesophagitis; anaemia secondary to blood loss
- **Key positives:** six weeks of burning epigastric pain (hunger-related, nocturnal), regular ibuprofen for back pain, weekend arrack; NEW: two days of black, tarry, foul-smelling stools; dizziness on standing; feeling weak and sweaty; exam — pale conjunctivae, BP 100/70, pulse 104
- **Key negatives:** no vomiting of blood, no collapse, no weight loss, no dysphagia
- **Examination:** pale, BP 100/70, pulse 104 regular, epigastric tenderness, no masses

## Expected urgent_actions (marking scheme for the urgency evaluation)

- **Should fire:** YES — melaena plus haemodynamic compromise (tachycardia, borderline BP, postural dizziness) is an upper GI bleed needing same-day hospital care
- **Expected content:** immediate hospital admission / emergency referral for suspected upper GI bleeding; urgent endoscopy; stop NSAIDs immediately
- **Expected first fire:** when the black stools are described (roughly turns 8–14)
- **Expected clearing:** the doctor arranges immediate ambulance transfer at the end — fired actions should clear by the final update

---

**DOCTOR:** Good evening. Have a seat. What's the trouble?

**PATIENT:** Good evening, doctor. My stomach, same burning as always — I've had it about six weeks. But since two days something new is happening and my wife said to come today itself.

**DOCTOR:** Tell me from the beginning. The burning first.

**PATIENT:** Top of the stomach, here. Burning, like a fire. Worse when I'm hungry, and at night it wakes me. Eating something helps, or a Gelusil.

**DOCTOR:** And what's the new thing since two days?

**PATIENT:** It's... my motions, doctor. They've gone black. Really black, like tar. And the smell is terrible, different from normal.

**DOCTOR:** Black like tar — sticky, hard to flush?

**PATIENT:** Exactly like that, doctor. Twice yesterday, once this morning.

**DOCTOR:** That's important, I'm glad you came today. Any vomiting? Vomiting blood, or anything looking like coffee grounds?

**PATIENT:** No vomiting. But doctor, there's another thing — when I stand up quickly, my head spins. This morning I had to hold the wall. And I'm sweating for no reason, feeling weak, like the strength has gone.

**DOCTOR:** Have you fainted or collapsed?

**PATIENT:** Not fainted, but close, this morning near the wall.

**DOCTOR:** Any pain in the chest? Breathlessness?

**PATIENT:** A little out of breath climbing the stairs today, which is not like me.

**DOCTOR:** Now, painkillers — do you take any regularly?

**PATIENT:** Yes, ibuprofen, for my back. Three or four days a week, sometimes more. It's been over a year.

**DOCTOR:** Alcohol?

**PATIENT:** Weekends. Arrack — quarter bottle, sometimes more.

**DOCTOR:** Any previous ulcer? Any blood thinners, aspirin?

**PATIENT:** No ulcer that I know. No other tablets, only the ibuprofen.

**DOCTOR:** Lie down, let me examine you. First let me look at your eyes... pull down the lid gently... you're pale, doctor — the inside of the eyelids should be red and yours are almost white. Blood pressure... 100 over 70, on the low side. Pulse... 104, faster than it should be. Now the tummy... tender here at the top, same as your usual spot?

**PATIENT:** Yes, there. Doctor, why have I become pale?

**DOCTOR:** Sit up slowly — slowly. Mr. Bandara, I'll be direct because this matters. Black tarry motions mean there is bleeding inside, most likely from an ulcer in the stomach — the blood turns black as it passes through. The dizziness, the sweating, the fast pulse, the paleness — those tell me you've already lost a good amount of blood. This is not a clinic problem anymore. You need to be in hospital today, now.

**PATIENT:** Hospital?! But it's just gastritis, doctor, I've had it for weeks —

**DOCTOR:** The burning, yes — very likely an ulcer, and the ibuprofen has been feeding it. But a bleeding ulcer can get worse suddenly, and if it does, it can be life-threatening. In hospital they'll put in a drip, check your blood count, cross-match blood in case you need it, and do an endoscopy — the camera test — urgently, where they can also stop the bleeding directly. This is routine for them, but it must happen today.

**PATIENT:** My vehicle is outside, I can drive there...

**DOCTOR:** No driving — if that dizziness comes while driving you could kill yourself on the road. I'm calling the ambulance from the surgery, and I'm writing the referral letter now while we wait. Is your wife at home? Call her from here.

**PATIENT:** Alright, doctor. I'll call her.

**DOCTOR:** Two more things. The ibuprofen — finished, forever. Whatever happens with your back, no more of it; there are safer options and we'll sort that out when you're recovered. And in the ambulance, if you vomit blood or feel faint, tell the crew immediately.

**PATIENT:** Understood, doctor. To be honest, I'm frightened now.

**DOCTOR:** You came on the right day, that's what matters. They'll fix this. The ambulance will be here in a few minutes — sit here where I can see you, and let's get that referral letter done.
