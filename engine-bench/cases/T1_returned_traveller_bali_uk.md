# T1 — Fever after a holiday in Bali (UK private GP) — TRAVEL CASE, DIAGNOSIS NEVER SPOKEN

**Fictional patient:** Ms. Chloe Barnes, 29, marketing manager
**Setting:** Private GP surgery, central London · **Speakers:** Doctor, Patient
**Target length:** 4–5 minutes
**Purpose:** benchmark case for v1.1 (Task 3i). A febrile returned traveller in
whom the target diagnosis, dengue, is never named by doctor or patient. The
patient frames it as flu. Travel is not volunteered; the doctor asks about it
only at turn 13, so the updates at turns 4, 8 and 12 show whether the CDS asks
about travel by itself.

Not part of the repo. Lives in v1.1-logs/travel/ and is read only by the bench.

## Expected clinical content (marking scheme)

- **Target diagnosis:** dengue fever, febrile phase, day 4, without warning signs.
- **Reasonable in the differential:** malaria (Bali is low risk, but malaria must
  be excluded in any febrile traveller from a risk area), chikungunya, Zika,
  typhoid or enteric fever, leptospirosis, viral exanthem, influenza, COVID-19.
- **Key positives:** sudden high fever; headache behind the eyes, worse on eye
  movement; severe muscle, joint and back pain; nausea, one vomit on day 1; loss
  of appetite, metallic taste; new blanching rash on day 4; many mosquito bites;
  returned from Bali 8 days ago; ibuprofen taken.
- **Key negatives:** no neck stiffness; no abdominal pain; no persistent vomiting;
  no bleeding; passing urine normally; drinking well; no respiratory symptoms;
  COVID test negative.
- **Examination:** T 38.7, HR 96, BP 118/76, warm hands, good circulation;
  blanching erythematous rash on chest, abdomen and arms; mild conjunctival
  injection; neck supple; chest clear; abdomen soft, non-tender, no liver or
  spleen felt.
- **Plan (turns 25 to 28):** same-day bloods (FBC for platelets and white cells,
  LFT, U&E, tests for imported infections); stop ibuprofen, paracetamol only;
  fluids; phone review with results tomorrow; warning signs to attend A&E,
  especially as the fever settles.

## Expected urgent_actions (marking scheme)

- **Proposed by Cowork, for the owner to decide:** silent. There are no warning
  signs, and same-day bloods with a review tomorrow is routine care.

**DOCTOR:** Hello, come and have a seat. What can I do for you today?

**PATIENT:** Hi. I've been really unwell for the last few days. I think it's some sort of flu, but it's knocked me flat, and my mum said I should get checked.

**DOCTOR:** I'm sorry to hear that. Tell me how it started.

**PATIENT:** It came on really suddenly on Saturday, so four days ago. I was fine in the morning, and by the afternoon I was shivering and boiling hot. I measured it that night and it was thirty-nine point four.

**DOCTOR:** That's a high temperature. Has it stayed high since?

**PATIENT:** Pretty much. It comes down a bit when I take tablets and then goes straight back up. I've been taking paracetamol and ibuprofen together to keep on top of it.

**DOCTOR:** What else have you noticed, apart from the temperature?

**PATIENT:** The headache is awful. It's right behind my eyes, and it even hurts to move my eyes around. And I ache everywhere: my back, my legs, my joints. It honestly feels like every bone in my body is aching.

**DOCTOR:** That sounds miserable. Any stiffness in your neck, or does bright light bother you?

**PATIENT:** My neck's fine, I can move it. Light bothers me a little with the headache, but I can watch telly.

**DOCTOR:** Any cough, sore throat, or trouble with your breathing?

**PATIENT:** No, nothing on my chest. I did a COVID test on Sunday and it was negative.

**DOCTOR:** Okay. Have you been abroad at all recently?

**PATIENT:** Yes, I got back from Bali eight days ago. I was there for two weeks with my sister. Do you think it's something from there?

**DOCTOR:** It's important to know, so thank you. Whereabouts in Bali were you, and did you get bitten by insects?

**PATIENT:** Mostly Ubud, inland, then a few days by the beach. I got eaten alive by mosquitoes, to be honest, day and evening. I had my travel jabs before I went, hepatitis and typhoid I think.

**DOCTOR:** Any upset stomach, being sick, or diarrhoea?

**PATIENT:** I felt sick the first day and was sick once, but not since. I'm just not hungry, and everything tastes a bit metallic.

**DOCTOR:** Any tummy pain? And any bleeding at all: from your gums when you brush your teeth, nosebleeds, or blood in your wee or poo?

**PATIENT:** No tummy pain. No bleeding that I've noticed. And I'm weeing normally. I'm drinking loads of water.

**DOCTOR:** Anything on your skin? Any rash?

**PATIENT:** That's the other thing. This morning I noticed red blotches on my chest and arms. It's a bit itchy.

**DOCTOR:** Let me examine you. ... Your temperature is thirty-eight point seven, your pulse is ninety-six and your blood pressure is one hundred and eighteen over seventy-six. Your hands are warm and your circulation is good. There's a red, blotchy rash over your chest, tummy and arms, and it fades when I press a glass on it. Your eyes are a little red. Your neck is supple, your chest is clear, and your tummy is soft and not tender. I can't feel your liver or spleen.

**PATIENT:** Is the rash a worry? My friend told me to check it with a glass.

**DOCTOR:** Your friend was right, and it does fade, which is reassuring. I think this is an infection you picked up on the trip from a mosquito bite. I'd like some blood tests today: a full blood count to check your platelets and white cells, your liver and kidneys, and specific tests for infections caught abroad. I'll mark them urgent so we have the results tomorrow.

**PATIENT:** Okay. Should I keep taking the ibuprofen?

**DOCTOR:** No. Please stop the ibuprofen and anything like it, such as aspirin. Take paracetamol only, at the normal dose, and keep drinking plenty. I'll ring you tomorrow with the results. The important thing is this: if you get tummy pain, keep vomiting, notice any bleeding from your gums or nose, or feel very drowsy or faint, go to A&E straight away. That can happen just as the temperature settles, so don't be falsely reassured when the fever goes.

**PATIENT:** That's really clear. Thank you. I'll stop the ibuprofen and wait for your call.
